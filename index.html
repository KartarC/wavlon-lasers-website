Sheet Cutting · ProCut
ProCut Series
3 kW — 12 kW · Fiber

Wavlon's entry-level daily-driver — gantry-style enclosed beam over an open machine bed. Available in 3015 and 4020 table formats. Ideal for job shops cutting mild steel, stainless, and aluminium up to 35 mm.

Explore ProCut
Sheet Cutting · PowerCut
PowerCut Series
3 kW — 12 kW · Fully Enclosed

Fully enclosed safety cabin with automatic pull-out single table. Higher acceleration (1.2G) and traverse speed (120 m/min) versus ProCut — cleaner shop environment, better optical safety.

Explore PowerCut
Sheet Cutting · UltraCut
UltraCut Series
3 kW — 20 kW · Dual Exchange

Wavlon's flagship platform — fully enclosed with dual exchangeable shuttle tables for continuous production. 3015 and 4020 frames, up to 20 kW standard (30 kW available on request).

Explore UltraCut
Tube Cutting · Double Chuck
TubeCut Double Chuck
1.5 kW — 12 kW · ⌀120 — 360 mm

Entry-to-intermediate fiber laser tube cutting on a double-chuck platform. Handles round, square, and rectangular profiles up to 360 mm on a 6 m bed — built for job shops and growing fabrication houses.

Explore TubeCut Double Chuck

TubeCut Triple Chuck
6 kW — 30 kW · ⌀360 — 680 mm

Heavy-duty triple-chuck tube cutting for large round and square sections on a 12 m bed. Built for structural steel fabricators and steel service centers running big HSS sections at scale.

Section 05 · HMI & Control

Cypcut.
Windows-native control.

The WD-3015E is driven by Cypcut, the de-facto Windows control suite for fiber laser cutting. CAD import, nesting, technology tables, live diagnostics — operators get a familiar Windows workflow on a 21.5″ portrait touchscreen, and engineers can drop into G-code when they need to.

  • Native CAD DXF / DWG / AI import, instant nest with material-aware spacing.
  • Technology tables Material × thickness × gas — every combination pre-tuned.
  • Live monitoring Cut quality, gas pressure, lens temp, beam dump — all on screen.
  • Network ready Drop files over LAN, queue jobs, push reports to MES.
